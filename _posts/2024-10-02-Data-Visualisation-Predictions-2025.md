As we look towards 2025, the landscape of data visualization and storytelling solutions is undergoing a significant transformation, driven mostly by important advances in artificial intelligence (AI). In this article, we explore three key predictions that highlight the emerging trends set to redefine how organizations interact with and visualize data, empower individuals to create data stories, and democratize information. From hybrid AI-human collaboration in data visualization to the integration of AI-powered solutions in decades-old platforms, these shifts are poised to enhance accessibility, improve decision-making, and foster a more data-literate workforce. To stay ahead in this rapidly evolving field, leaders must adapt to these technological advancements and embrace the opportunities they present to their organizations. As you read through these predictions, consider how your organization can leverage these innovations to drive success and stay competitive in the years to come.

<h3>Prediction 1: Increased AI-Human Collaboration in Creating Data Visualization</h3>
By 2025, AI-driven data visualization tools will become more common in data analytics and business intelligence. Tools like OpenAI's ChatGPT and Anthropic's Claude, along with AI systems that include human oversight, are changing how organizations create and interpret data visualizations. These AI systems can quickly produce complex visual content, making advanced data tools more accessible to a broader audience. However, the design aspects still benefit from human input. This trend presents both benefits and challenges. AI can take over routine tasks, allowing designers to focus on refining visualizations for clarity and impact. However, with more people using these tools, it's important to ensure that visualizations adhere to best practices, data
accuracy is maintained, and privacy concerns are addressed. To make the most of these tools,
organizations should develop frameworks that combine AI efficiency with human oversight. This
could involve setting guidelines for data visualization practices, continuing to invest in data and
dashboard design training, and ensuring strong data privacy measures. With this approach,
organizations can produce visualizations that are accurate, clear, and secure.


<h4>Implications for Leaders / C-Level Executives:</h4>
You can enhance your team's productivity by using AI for routine visualization tasks, allowing your data practitioners to focus on improving the quality of reports. It's important to establish guidelines to ensure data visualizations solutions follow design best practices and comply with data privacy regulations. Regularly review your team’s training needs and data policies to protect sensitive information.

<h4>Implications for Data / Business Analysts:</h4>
AI tools will make it easier to create data visualizations products and dashboards, even if you’re
not deeply technical. But you’ll need to ensure that the data is accurate and that data
visualizations you create are well-designed. Be aware of your organization’s data privacy policies,
and help others use these tools effectively while maintaining high standards of data management
and design.

<h3>Prediction 2: Embedded and LLM-Powered Features Will Further Shape Data Visualisation Solutions</h3>
AI-driven tools like Microsoft’s Copilot in PowerBI and Einstein Copilot for Tableau, along with
their integration into traditionally static platforms like Power BI or Tableau within PowerPoint, will
continue to change how organizations approach data visualization. These advancements allow
teams to interact with data using natural language and, through its embedding, enable
executives to engage with data directly during presentations. Instead of relying on complex
queries or static reports, users can now ask questions in plain language and receive immediate,
actionable visualizations that fit smoothly into their workflow. This is particularly valuable for
larger organizations, where handling vast amounts of data can be time-consuming. With these
tools, employees at all levels can access and understand data without needing advanced
technical skills. However, the convenience of AI also brings the need to ensure that the data and
insights generated are accurate, well designed and relevant.


<h4>Implications for Leaders / C-Level Executives:</h4>
The integration of AI tools into traditional platforms
like PowerPoint means your team can create data visualizations more efficiently. This could allow
you to perform data exploration during meetings, helping you make faster, more informed
decisions. However, it's important to ensure that your team is trained to make data presented in
an accurate and well-designed way.

<h4>Implications for Data / Business Analysts:</h4>
With features and embedded capabilities in platforms
like PowerPoint, you can create interactive visualizations more easily and present them directly in
a familiar format. This could be helpful in sharing insights more effectively during meetings with
c-level executives. However, you'll need to ensure the accuracy of the data and the clarity of the
visualizations from a design perspective.

<h3>Prediction 3: The Modest Growth of Web-Based Tools Amidst AI Advancements</h3>
In 2025, web-based tools like Observable and RATH may see some growth, but they are unlikely
to overtake established platforms like PowerBI, Tableau, and Qlik. While AI is making it simpler for
people to generate code for data visualizations, these web-based platforms are still being used
more as niche tools rather than mainstream solutions. For example, a business analyst might use
AI to quickly generate code, then refine it in one of the many web-based tools available. But for most organizations, larger
platforms like PowerBI and Tableau, which are more integrated into business workflows, will likely
remain the go-to options. As AI continues to simplify the development of data visualizations, the
major players in the field will probably continue to dominate. Web-based tools will still have their
place, especially for specific tasks or for those who prefer more flexibility in their work.


<h4>Implications for Leaders / C-Level Executives:</h4>
It's important to recognize that platforms like
PowerBI, Tableau, and Qlik will remain the primary choices for data visualization solutions in
medium to large size organizations. You should continue to invest in these established platforms
to maintain consistency and reliability across your data operations, including training your teams
on how these tools could be fully leveraged. However, stay open to exploring niche web-based
tools for specific projects or teams that may benefit from their flexibility.

<h4>Implications for Data / Business Analysts:</h4>
You may find opportunities to use web-based tools like
Observable for specific tasks or projects, especially if you’re comfortable with more flexible,
coding-intensive environments. However, if you work in medium to large organizations, you will
likely continue to be centered around platforms like PowerBI, Tableau, and Qlik, which are more
embedded in organizational processes. It’s important to stay proficient in these major tools.
Balance your exploration of new tools with the practical demands of your day-to-day work,
ensuring that your contributions remain aligned with the broader technology stack of your
organization.
